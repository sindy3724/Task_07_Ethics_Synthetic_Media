# Riverbend Ledger: Synthetic Media Policy

**Applies to:** all staff, freelancers, contractors, interns, and volunteers producing content for the Riverbend Ledger or on its behalf.
**Owner:** Managing Editor. **Version:** 1.0. **Review date:** 12 months after adoption, or sooner after any incident.

*The Riverbend Ledger is a fictional composite newsroom (22 staff, nonprofit, local audience). See README.*

**Definition.** "Synthetic media" means audio, video, or images in which a voice, face, body, or scene has been generated or materially altered by AI so that it depicts something that was not actually recorded. It excludes ordinary editing (cropping, color correction, noise reduction, captioning, transcription).

**The one rule behind the rest:** *Our audience must never be led to believe a real person said or did something they did not, and must never be unsure whether what they are seeing was recorded.*

---

## 1. Permitted uses (no special approval beyond Section 7's tier)

You may use synthetic media for:

1. **Illustrative graphics** that depict no real person and are not photo-realistic news imagery (e.g., an abstract image for an explainer on property taxes).
2. **Accessibility**: text-to-speech reading of our own published articles in a *clearly labeled, non-person-like* synthetic voice.
3. **Translation audio** of our own reporting, labeled, reviewed by a fluent human before release.
4. **Internal drafts and mockups** that never leave the organization (storyboards, pitch visuals).
5. **Reconstructions that are plainly diagrammatic** (e.g., a stylized animation of a traffic collision sequence, with no faces and a visible "illustration" label), **Amber tier** (see Section 7).

If your proposed use is not on this list and not in Section 2, it is **not permitted without Amber review**. When in doubt, ask; do not assume.

## 2. Prohibited uses (refused regardless of intent, disclosure, or consent)

1. **Depicting a real, identifiable person saying or doing something they did not say or do**, including "for illustration," "satire," or "as a hypothetical." Not permitted even with labeling.
2. **Voice or face cloning of any real person**, including staff, sources, public officials, and deceased people, *except* under the narrow consent exception in Section 3.
3. **Synthetic photo-realistic imagery presented as news photography**, including "recreating" a scene we lacked a photographer for.
4. **Synthetic "eyewitnesses," "residents," "sources," or "experts"**, including composites, even if labeled.
5. **Altering recorded media in a way that changes its meaning** (e.g., regenerating a quote's audio to "fix" a mumble).
6. **Any intimate, sexual, or sexualized synthetic depiction** of any real person.
7. **Any synthetic depiction of a minor** that resembles a real child, and any use of children's voices or likenesses.
8. **Using synthetic media to test, probe, or "demonstrate" a deception on the public** without Editor-in-Chief approval and a published disclosure (see Section 8, Refusal).
9. **Generating content to simulate audience or community support** (comments, testimonials, reviews, petitions).

Prohibited uses cannot be unlocked by consent forms, editor sign-off, or deadline pressure.

## 3. Consent workflow

Consent applies only to the **narrow exception**: a staff member's own voice or likeness used for an approved Section 1 use (e.g., a reporter's cloned voice reading translations of their own stories).

1. **Who obtains it:** the requesting editor. **Who documents it:** the Managing Editor, in a central log.
2. **Written, specific consent** naming: the exact use, the output formats, the channels, and the duration.
3. **Validity:** 12 months maximum, then renewal. Consent **lapses automatically** when the person leaves the organization or changes role.
4. **Revocation:** the person may revoke at any time by email to the Managing Editor. Within **5 business days**, the Ledger stops new use, removes the voice/likeness model and source recordings from our systems, and takes down published pieces where feasible.
5. **No coercion:** consent is never a condition of employment, a freelance contract, or access to a story. Declining has no consequence.
6. **Sources and members of the public:** we do **not** use consent to clone them. See Section 2.2.

## 4. Disclosure standards

1. **Every** synthetic element must be disclosed in **two** ways: a **visible/audible label** in the content itself and a **description-field note** where the platform allows.
2. **Audio:** begin with a spoken line, e.g., "This audio was generated by AI from our written article." Repeat at the midpoint for pieces over 2 minutes.
3. **Video/images:** a persistent on-screen "AI-generated" label. Not a fading caption, and not in a corner outside platform crop areas.
4. **Plain language only.** Not "enhanced," "assisted," or "digital." Use "AI-generated."
5. **Re-share and excerpt design.** Because labels get stripped, we **avoid producing synthetic content that is persuasive or meaningful on its own** (see Section 7 review question 3). Spoken in-audio disclosure is preferred over overlay-only disclosure.
6. **A label does not make a prohibited use permitted.**

## 5. Provenance requirements

1. Outputs are produced with tools that support **content credentials (C2PA or equivalent)** where available; credentials are **retained on export**.
2. Where tools do not support credentials, the Managing Editor keeps a **provenance record**: tool, version, prompt/source text, date, creator, approver, consent record ID.
3. All synthetic outputs are stored in a **single registry** (a shared folder with a log), so any clip can be identified as ours or not ours.
4. We **publish a public "Synthetic Media" page** listing what we have produced and how to report suspected misuse of our name or staff's likenesses.
5. **We do not rely on provenance as proof.** The absence of credentials on a clip does not show it is fake; the presence does not show it is true.

## 6. Roles

- **Requester:** proposes use; completes the request form.
- **Approving Editor:** an editor *other than* the requester.
- **Managing Editor:** owns the policy, consent log, registry, and incident response.
- **Editor-in-Chief:** final authority on Red-tier questions and on this policy.

## 7. Review and approval

**Tier GREEN (no review):** Section 1 items 1, 2, 4. Log it in the registry.
**Tier AMBER (second-editor review before production):** Section 1 items 3, 5, and any use not listed.
**Tier RED:** anything in Section 2. Cannot be approved.

The Approving Editor must answer these questions *in writing* before sign-off:

1. Does it depict, imitate, or evoke a real, identifiable person? *(If yes: stop; see Section 2.)*
2. Could a reasonable viewer take it for a recording of real events?
3. If the label were stripped, would the clip still be accurate and non-misleading on its own?
4. Is the use necessary, or merely convenient? Would plain text, a human voice, or an illustration do the same job?
5. What is the worst plausible misuse of this specific clip, and have we reduced it?

**Approval is recorded** in the registry with the reviewer's name. **Deadline pressure is not a reason to skip review**; if review can't happen, the piece is not produced.

## 8. Refusal: when we say no even to permitted uses

The Ledger **declines** to produce synthetic media when:

- the story involves **an ongoing election, ballot measure, or criminal case** and the clip could be mistaken for evidence or testimony;
- the reviewer **cannot answer** the Section 7 questions with confidence;
- a **crisis, emergency, or breaking event** is unfolding, where unverified synthetic content could be mistaken for reporting;
- the **only reason** is cost or speed;
- the subject of the story, or anyone depicted, **has objected**;
- the Approving Editor and Requester **disagree**. Disagreement defaults to refusal.

**Refusal is a normal outcome, and no one is penalized for raising it.** Any staff member may refuse an assignment that appears to breach this policy.

## 9. Incident response

An **incident** is any of: a Ledger synthetic clip misused, stripped of its label, or mistaken for real; an unauthorized synthetic depiction of Ledger staff or content; or a breach of this policy.

1. **Report** to the Managing Editor immediately (target: within 2 hours of discovery). Reporting in good faith is never punished; concealment is.
2. **Contain (within 24 hours):** take down our copy; request removal from platforms; preserve evidence (originals, registry entry, URLs, timestamps).
3. **Assess:** was it our breach, downstream misuse, or a third-party fake using our name?
4. **Notify (within 48 hours):** affected individuals; for material cases, a **public correction or notice** on the Synthetic Media page. Do not hide it.
5. **Remediate:** fix the cause (process, tool, training), update the registry, and, if staff misconduct, handle under HR policy.
6. **Review:** a short written post-mortem within 14 days, shared with all staff.

## 10. Training and review

- All staff complete a 30-minute briefing at adoption and annually.
- Freelancers must acknowledge this policy before their first assignment.
- The policy is reviewed every 12 months and after every Red/Amber incident.

# Task 07: The Ethics of Synthetic Representation

## What this task is

Research Task 7 asks for two things:

- **Phase A**: ethical analysis of synthetic voice/face media, reasoned outward from a hands-on artifact along the axes of truth, consent, context, and scale, plus a survey of mitigations and where each breaks.
- **Phase B**: a policy an organization could adopt, with an honest stress-test of where it fails.

## Organizational context chosen, and why

**The Riverbend Ledger** is a fictional composite: a 22-person nonprofit local newsroom serving a mid-sized county, with a small audience-engagement team and a freelance pool. I chose it because:

1. Its product is trust. A synthetic voice or face that misleads costs it its whole value.
2. Its constraints are easy to describe: small staff, no legal department, deadline pressure, and a temptation to use cheap synthetic audio/video for efficiency.
3. It is a standard setting, which makes it a fair test of whether I can write a non-boilerplate policy for it (see the portability note in the limitations).

## Brief reflection: what surprised me

The most surprising result of writing the policy was how little of it depends on technology. Almost every enforceable rule turned out to be about **who may authorize what** and **who is accountable when it goes wrong**, not about labels or detectors. The mitigations that sound most technical (watermarks, provenance, detection) were the ones I could rely on least.
